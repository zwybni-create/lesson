4. Reading: the procedures at a glance

Diagnostic hysteroscopy looks at the cervical canal and cavity and is often done in the outpatient (“office”) setting with a thin scope, sometimes without a speculum (vaginoscopic approach). It is usually planned after menstruation, when the lining is thin. Operative hysteroscopy treats the problem during the same visit (“see-and-treat”). Hysteroresectoscopy uses a resectoscope to remove larger lesions such as submucous fibroids or a septum, usually in the operating theater under anesthesia.

  • PrepareConsent, pregnancy test, anesthesia or analgesia, lithotomy position.
  • ExposeSpeculum, clean cervix, tenaculum if needed.
  • DilateHegar dilators until the sheath passes.
  • EnterIntroduce the scope under direct vision; start inflow.
  • InspectCanal, cavity walls, fundus, both tubal ostia.
  • TreatBiopsy, polypectomy, resection in strips, coagulate bleeders.
  • FinishRemove chips, withdraw slowly, record fluid deficit.
Safety points
  • Never advance the scope without a clear view.
  • If the cavity suddenly collapses or you see bowel or omentum, suspect perforation: stop and call for help.
  • Keep a running tally of the fluid deficit and tell the team aloud.
  • Use the correct fluid for the type of current: electrolyte-free fluid for monopolar, saline for bipolar.

Talking to the patient

“We will look inside your womb with a thin camera. You may feel cramping, like a period.”
“Please tell me if the pain becomes stronger. We can pause at any time.”
“You may have light bleeding and cramps for a few days. Call us if you have fever or heavy bleeding.”
